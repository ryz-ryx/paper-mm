# Hyperliquid funding-carry backtest (research only, no trading)

Generated by `analysis/funding_carry.py` from `analysis/funding_data/` (public `fundingHistory` + 1 h `candleSnapshot`, POST `https://api.hyperliquid.xyz/info`,
window 2026-07-10 18:00 to 2026-10-08 18:00 UTC = 90 days = 2,160 hourly funding prints per coin). Accounting is unit-tested
(`analysis/test_funding_carry.py`). Nothing here places orders or touches the paper-mm code.

## Bottom line

- **Delta-neutral (DN) carry earned roughly 15-24 % annualised on $200 in both periods, with fees a small part of it** (6 bps maker / 18 bps taker per cycle against
  100-290 bps of funding per cycle). But the result is **not evidence for the 10/20/40 % entry thresholds or the 5 % exit**: the exit level sits below Hyperliquid's
  baseline funding (10.95 % APR, which is the median coin-hour in every period), so almost no position ever exits on the signal. Positions are held about 300-720 hours,
  the book is ~95-100 % invested, and the "strategy" is effectively *hold the five highest-funding coins you can hedge*. The three entry thresholds give nearly the same
  answer, and the dev "best" threshold differs between fee sets and signals by a few points of return that are well inside noise.
- **Unhedged (short perp only) is a price bet that happens to receive funding.** Dev results swing from +61 % to -127 % annualised depending on signal and threshold; in the
  hold-out the dev-selected raw-signal variant made +4.5 % with a -38 % drawdown, and the smoothed variant lost 264 % annualised (price bps -1,753 per cycle against
  +206 bps of funding). Funding does not pay for being short a coin that rallies.
- **Sample size is tiny.** 14-24 cycles in dev, 5-7 in the hold-out (mostly forced closes at the end of the period), from 5 slots. Reported Sharpe ratios are not meaningful (see below).

## Design (fixed before any result was seen, except where noted)

| Item | Choice |
|---|---|
| Universe | every Hyperliquid perp that returned funding **and** candles (coverage table below) |
| Signals | `raw`: latest hourly print x 24 x 365 (the literal task rule). `ma24`: trailing 24-print mean x 24 x 365 (smoothed; added because single prints are noisy) |
| Entry / exit | enter when signal > T % APR for T in {10, 20, 40}; exit when signal < 5 % APR; positive funding only (perp is the short leg) |
| Modes | **UNH** short perp only. **DN** short perp + long hedge leg modelled as a perp-only proxy: hedge price move cancels the short exactly, so price P&L = 0 (no basis, no hedge-availability or borrow limits) |
| Fees | maker/maker 1.5 bps per side = 3 bps round trip per leg; taker/taker 4.5 per side = 9 round trip per leg. **DN pays them on both legs** (6 / 18 bps) - a one-leg-only reading would add 3 / 9 bps back per cycle |
| Portfolio | $200, 5 slots of $40 (UNH: $40 short; DN: $20 short + $20 hedge). Highest signal first. No compounding, idle cash earns 0. Max drawdown is on the hourly mark-to-market equity |
| Timing | decision on the print of hour h; execution at the **open of hour h+1**; funding credited for prints h+2 ... exit-decision hour only (the print that coincides with an execution instant is never credited); funding per print = rate x notional x open(h)/entry |
| Period split | dev = first 1,440 h (60 d), hold-out = last 720 h (30 d), each simulated flat-start with a forced close at its end; the signal history before the hold-out start is used only as past information |
| Threshold freezing | per (signal, mode, fee set) the dev threshold with the best dev annualised return (>= 10 dev cycles) is frozen in code and **only that one** is run on the hold-out. **Disclosure:** the cycle floor was first 20; I lowered it to 10 after seeing that `ma24` had only 14-15 dev cycles (dev-only information). The first run showed the `raw` hold-out rows before that change; the change did not alter the `raw` selections and `ma24` had no hold-out result at that time, so no hold-out information drove it |
| Sharpe | annualised from daily equity changes (x sqrt 365) |

## Reading the results

1. **Fees are not the issue.** DN pays 6 (maker) or 18 (taker) bps per cycle; gross funding is 118-290 bps per cycle. Taker instead of maker costs about 1-2 points of annual return.
2. **Thresholds and the exit rule hardly bind.** Slot utilisation is 95-100 %, mean holds are 300-720 h, and a large share of cycles end by the forced close at the period end (see the `forced close %` column). Differences between T = 10, 20 and 40 in dev are single-digit points of annual return from a handful of different coin picks.
3. **Selection by the highest print.** Slots are filled with the coins with the largest signal at that moment (extreme prints). Part of the later funding comes from the same high-funding regime persisting, which is the real effect being captured, but nothing here proves it persists out of sample beyond these two windows.
4. **Sharpe and drawdown for DN are artifacts.** With a perfect proxy hedge the equity curve is funding accrual only, so daily volatility is tiny (Sharpe 20-30, max drawdown 0.02-0.05 %). Real DN risk - basis moves, hedge unavailability, liquidation of the short leg during a squeeze, funding flipping negative, exchange risk - is not in this model. The unhedged rows show what price risk looks like (max drawdown 21-54 % in dev, 38-57 % in the hold-out).
5. **Cycle counts are too small for inference.** 5-7 hold-out cycles from 5 slots; no confidence intervals are attached because the cycles are neither independent (same regime) nor numerous.

## Other caveats

- Fills are at the hourly open with no spread, impact or partial fills; a $20-$40 order is far below any book, but a maker fill at the open is assumed to happen, and funding spikes coincide with volatile books.
- Funding is assumed to be paid on notional x open price at each print (Hyperliquid uses the oracle price at that time; the candle open is a close proxy).
- DN assumes a hedge exists for every coin; most Hyperliquid perps have no liquid spot market, and cross-venue hedges add transfer, venue and margin costs not modelled.
- 1x collateral per perp, 5 slots: no liquidation is modelled in UNH; the -1,753 bps/cycle price loss in the `ma24` hold-out is a 17.5 % loss on that slot's notional.
- Two non-overlapping 60/30-day windows of a single quarter are one market regime, not a sample of regimes.
